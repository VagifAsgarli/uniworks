Exercises 1–2: SHA-256 Kernel
Notebook: work in Google Colab. Choose a Julia runtime with a
T4 GPU, then run the setup cells. Download the track A
wordlist.txt and your hashes_<ID>.txt from ORTUS.
Start from the kernel skeleton in the notebook; it keeps the SHA-
256 state in registers.
Complete the round function (Σ, Ch, Maj, wrapping UInt32 additions).
Verify the kernel against the NIST test vectors for "abc" and the
empty string "".

Exercise 3: Dictionary Attack
Copy the wordlist to the device; for each (salt, target) pair, hash
every candidate and record the index of the match.
Dataset: your hashes_<ID>.txt, 200 (salt, SHA-256(salt ‖
password)) pairs. Some passwords are dictionary words, some are
words changed by a rule, and some are random.
The wordlist and the passwords are synthetic.
Report the hash rate (H/s) and the number of recovered
passwords.
Extension: record all matches with the scan and compaction
pattern of lecture 7.

Exercises 4–5: Rules and Defence
Add simple mangling rules (capitalise, leet substitution, append
digits) to the candidate generator; measure how coverage and
candidate count grow.
Re-run against targets stored with iterated SHA-256 (4 096
iterations), a stand-in for key stretching, and measure the drop in
password checks per second. PBKDF2 and Argon2 are discussed,
not implemented.

Session Outcome
By the end of the session you should have:
1. Your verified SHA-256 kernel and its hash rate.
2. Dictionary-attack results: recovery count and H/s.
3. The effect of rules on coverage.
4. A defensive recommendation: a password policy and a storage
function with parameters, justified by your measured single-GPU
hash rate.
Reading: FIPS 180-4; OWASP Password Storage Cheat Sheet.
