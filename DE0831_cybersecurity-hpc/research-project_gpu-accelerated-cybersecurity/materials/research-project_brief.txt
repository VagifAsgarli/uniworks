Requirements
Runs reproducibly on a stated GPU: your own device or Google
Colab. State the GPU model, and keep every run within one
session’s limits.
A baseline to compare against: a tuned CPU implementation, a
library, or an unoptimised GPU version.
A measurement: throughput, scaling, or a cost/accuracy curve,
reported with the timing method of lecture 7 (warm-up,
CUDA.@elapsed, repeated samples).
A defensive or analytical conclusion: the implications of the
measurements for a real security decision.
Reproducibility: Project.toml, Manifest.toml, a script or notebook
that reproduces the runs, and a README.

The Report (6–10 Pages)
1. Problem and measurable objective: the security context and the target metric.
2. Approach: the GPU design, the memory strategy, and the decomposition.
3. Baseline and methodology: the comparison baseline and measurement procedure.
4. Results: benchmark tables and scaling plots.
5. Security discussion: the implications for a defender or analyst; limitations; ethical/legal notes for
the domain.
6. Reproducibility appendix: hardware, versions, and execution instructions.
The 6–10 pages do not include the title page, table of contents, references and appendices.

Authorised Use
Every project operates only on self-generated or instructor-
provided data and on systems for which you hold authorisation
(your own device or your own Colab session). No project may
target, probe, or collect data from any external system, network, or
individual.
A small, well-measured result is preferable to an ambitious
implementation with no baseline.
