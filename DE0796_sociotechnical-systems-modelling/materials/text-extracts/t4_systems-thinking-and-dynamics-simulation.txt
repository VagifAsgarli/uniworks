Systems Thinking

Causal Loops Diagrams show the
cause and effect relationships between
the variables of a system. There are two
basic feedback loops – balancing and
reinforcing loops. Balancing loops tend
to keep the system in its current stage
and reinforcing loops tend to compound
change in one direction.

Kim, Daniel H. (1999), Introduction to Systems Thinking,
Pegasus Communications, Inc.

System Dynamics is a methodology for
studying and managing complex feedback
systems, such as one finds in business and
other social systems.

Bob Williams and Bill Harris, System Dynamics
Methodology. WK Kellogg Foundation, 2005.

Recommendations for CLD drawing


  Pay attention to:
   Simplify the model as much as possible;
   Respect important and critical events, influences, effects and
    relationships only;
   Design step by step;
   Don't be afraid of feedbacks;
   Reinforcing loops must be balanced, otherwise the system
    will be unstable;
   Organize the diagrams to minimize crossed lines;
   Maintain the model transparent;
   Iterate and re-draw and verifiy your diagram many times to
    find the best layout;
   Split model in interrelated domains (sub-systems) if
    necessary.

System Dynamics (SD)*


   System Dynamics - a methodology to create descriptive
    models that focus on the identification of causal relations
    influencing the creation and evolution of the issues being
    investigated. Aimed for studying and managing complex
    feedback systems, such as business and other social systems.
    Its main pillars are feedback loops, delays and nonlinearity
    through the explicit representation of stocks and flows. SD is a
    continuous deterministic modelling technique.



  Roberts, N., Andersen, D. F., Deal, R. M., Garet, M. S., and Shaffer, W.
  A. (1983 ) Introduction to Computer simulation: The System Dynamics
  Approach, Addison Wesley Publishing Company, London Amsterdam
  Don Mills Ontario Sydney.

From CLD to Stocks and Flows Diagrams



    Stocks represent the accumulations existing in a system and
     characterize the state of the system. Stocks increase due to
     inflows, and they decrease due to outflows. Stocks are
     responsible for the delays as they accumulate the difference
     between inflows and outflows. Basically, stocks and flows
     represent differential equations.

SD modelling step by step

The SD modelling approach assumes the following sequence:

 Identifying independent stocks or accumulations (levels) in the system
    and their inflows and outflows;
   Formulating a behavioural model (stock-and flow/causal feedback
    structure) capable of reproducing the dynamic problem of concern;
   Verification of stock and flows model through review of differential
    equations generated, test sample and visual shape;
   Experiments with stocks and flow model and validation the results
    comparing with real system data or the models of other authors or
    opinions of other experts;
   Deriving understandings and applicable policy insights from the
    resulting model;
   Implementing changes resulting from model-based understandings
    and insights;
   Enhance the model receiving feadback of users.

Conclusions


  Causal Loop Diagrams (CLD) design - systems thinking, which can
  represent your even hidden ideas about the system;

  Systemic Gap – transition from CLD to stocks and flows model (SD);

  SD stocks and flows diagrams design - engineering work that is
  based on the designer's experience;

  Verification - always first verify the model trend and check the
  boundary conditions;

  Scaling - selection the wrong scale can lead to completely wrong
  visual verification results;

  Structure - SD model structure is more important than the objects of
  model design. Tuning can be long and annoying.
