The Unified Modeling Language (UML) is a general-purpose modelling language used in software engineering and aimed to standardize visualisation of the goal system design;

Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN)
Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN)
Business Process Model and Notation (2005) has become the de-facto standard for graphical business processes diagrams. Version 2.0 of BPMN2 was released in January 2011 and maintained by the Object Management Group (OMG). BPMN is also ratified as ISO 19510. It is intended to be used directly by the stakeholders who design, manage and realize business processes, but at the same time be precise enough to allow BPMN diagrams to be translated into software process components. BPMN has an easy-to-use flowchart-like notation that is independent of any particular implementation environment.

Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN)*
BPMN2 is not a methodology, but a graphical notation for universal diagramming. Diagrams provide parallelization of processes both by feature (Pool, Swimlanes) and by condition (Gateways). Each process consists of Activities (tasks, procedures) connected by both sequence and data/message flows. Diagrams can include a variety of data objects. Each process can have both a start and an end event, and a set of different intermediate events (Events) can be used. Conditional transitions are provided by the Gateway set (OR, AND, XOR).
About the Business Process Model and Notation Specification Version 2.0. https://www.omg.org/spec/BPMN/2.0/

Conclusions
Creating a conceptual model begins with the generalization and summarization of ideas at the macro level, determining the relationship and influence of concept map elements;
The causal relations of the conceptual model are represented by Causal loops diagram (CLD), clearly showing the feedbacks that can cause both reinforcing and stabilizing effects of the goal system;
In order to fully describe the functionality of the conceptual model, a mechanism for representing process parallelization is required, both by feature and by condition;
The above capabilities are provided equally well by both UML and BPMN2 techniques;
RTU, 2026
=== Slide 37 ===
37
Conclusions*
For the visualization of conceptual models of sociotechnical systems, it is preferable to use the BPMN2 technique, which ensures better transparency of the model and, therefore, deeper involvement of stakeholders in the design. This is critical for the preparation of user requirements and the subsequent successful implementation of the project;
When creating a model, a reasonable and transparent level of abstraction must always be maintained, creating separate models for embedded tasks, as well as drawing the necessary swimlanes content in detail;
Special attention should be paid to the verification and validation of the model, because the errors left in the conceptual model will inevitably lead to the fatal implementation of the project.
