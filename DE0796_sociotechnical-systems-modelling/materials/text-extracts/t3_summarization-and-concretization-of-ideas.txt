=== Slide 5 ===
Representation of the Concept
5
“A concept model is a high-level description of an application:
It enumerates all concepts (ideas) in the application that a user can encounter
Describes how those concepts (ideas) relate to each other
And explains how those concepts (ideas) fit into task that users perform with the application”
Johnson & Henderson, Conceptual Models, 2012
 
Recommendations:
Keep it simple and focused, and conceptual
Do not describe implementation and interface specifications
Describe utility
Tim Sheiner, The Conceptual Model, 2013

Philosophy of Conceptual modelling
7
Relies on:
Assumptions – uncertainties or beliefs about the real world being modelled
Simplifications – ways of reducing the complexity of the model maintaining sufficient transparency of the system
Done by:
Removing of components and interconnections that have little effect on model accuracy
Representing more simply the components and interconnections while maintaining a satisfactory level of model accuracy
 
Conceptual modelling, De La Salle University-Manila, 2011. https://www.slideshare.net/benghuid/conceptual-modeling

Philosophy of Conceptual modelling*
8
Conceptual models are essential to knowledge production and decision support
They are not set in stone: they depend on available knowledge and the objectives pursued

Sources of knowledge:
	Expert knowledge;
	Scientific and technical literature;
	Observations (measures);
	Experiments;
	Simulation.
 Jean-Noel Aubertot, Why modelling? Conceptual modelling, Summer School, Endure, 2016

Concept mapping
Concept map:
A technique for visualizing the relationships among different concepts (ideas);
A diagram showing the relationships based on arrows. The relationships can be articulated in linking phrase, e.g., “results in”, “contributes to”, “giver rise to” etc.
Can be used as a tool to increase meaningful learning as well as to represent the expert knowledge of individuals and teams.
Have their origin in constructivism (knowledge constructing by learners applying previous knowledge as background). “The most important single factor influencing learning is what the learner already knows. Ascertain this and teach accordingly (Novak, 1984)”.
Novak, J.D. & Gowin, D. B., Learning How to Learn, Cambridge University Press, 1984.

=== Slide 24 ===
24
Rationale
Rationale
(Characterization of the situation. Deficiencies and shortcomings of the existing solution. Justification why changes to the existing solution or a new solution/product are needed)
For example:
"As the costs of operating vehicles and the flow of tourists increase, it is necessary to increase the throughput of transport routes. It is necessary to increase the efficiency of each trip. It is necessary to reduce the volume of ticket sales on the bus, using cash and credit cards, which delay the travel time. The existing ticketing system TANGO does not provide this. The most financially effective solution is the development and implementation of unified transport electronic tickets-wallet for based on RFID cards."

Goal
Goal
(The goal of implementing the sociotechnical system. Only one goal that can be achieved. Which of the problems identified in the Rationale will be solved?)
For example:
"Development and implementation of TANGO PRO, a pre-paid RFID card-based bus passenger ticketing system."

Objectives
Objectives
(Several sub-goals, the joint achievement of which ensures the achievement of the systemic Goal)

"O1. Ensure convenient and accessible registration of the customer's e-ticket account and replenishment with the required amount of money;
O2. Create a unified infrastructure for TANGO PRO RFID e-ticket authorization (bus, self-service payment stands, cash desks, Internet);
O3. Introduce a flexible system of discounts for different population groups;
O4. Involve TANGO PRO in a unified network of different modes of city transport."

=== Slide 48 ===
48
Change management: Risks
Risks
(Description of possible risks of system development and implementation. Both internal and external risks are identified. Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats (SWOT) analysis can be done. It is also desirable to describe the possible effects of hidden and unanticipated factors. Development of procedures that allow identifying critical design failures and successfully stopping the project by implementing a roll-back mechanism in order to complete the project with minimal losses for stakeholders.)
For example:
"Since TANGO PRO has a link with the bus company's staff accounting and bus fleet management system, time delays in data exchange are possible. Internet and mobile network coverage may cause interference. The system must comply with the requirements of changes in AML regulations. Changes in the standardization of RFID frequency bands at the national level should be monitored. Alternatives should be provided in the selection of RFID equipment manufacturers. Compliance with GDPR requirements must be monitored.”
RTU, 2026
=== Slide 49 ===
49
Change management: Verification/Validation
Verification/Validation
(What methods will be used for verification and validation procedures during system development and exploitation?)
For example:
"Verification of concepts in interactive mode is carried out in a group of interested parties, which includes representatives of municipalities, passenger transportation company, financial institutions and organizations representing social groups. The verification of technical solutions is performed by checking the border values and the random values of the parameters. Validation is performed by comparing system performance results with system requirements. Verification and validation are performed after each design step.”

=== Slide 50 ===
50
Change management: Sustainability development
Sustainability development
(The impact of the system on society and the surrounding environment is assessed, which in the Triple Bottom Line (TBL) model determines the sustainability of the system and its development. The impact on business is included in the assessment of the impact on society. Possible changes in the development of sustainability during the exploitation of the system are predicted.)
For example:
"TANGO PRO will have a neutral impact on the environment. A positive impact on the society is expected, as it will be more convenient to pay for tickets, but the e-ticket account can be replenished at any time. The sustainability risk can be caused by the influence of financial institutions, which will want to transform e-tickets into payment cards."
