Systems*
A social system is a complex set of human relationships interacting in many ways. Within an organization, the social system includes all the people in it and their relationships to each other and to the outside world. The social system does not have boundaries...it exchanges goods, ideas, culture, etc. with the environment around it. The environment of social systems includes other social systems.


     Luhmann, Niklas (1982). "The World Society as a Social System," in International Journal of General Systems Vol. 8, No. 1.

Sociotechnical Systems (STS) Theory–Historical Background
STS theory was established by Eric Trist, Fred Emery (1951) and a group of the researchers of the Tavistock Institute of Human Relations in London as a new form of work restructuring model to solve the extensive fieldwork in a number of British coal fields.

RTU, 2026
=== Slide 16 ===
Sociotechnical System (STS)*
Sociotechnical system (STS) is the system that contain both social (human-related or natural) and technical (non-human) aspects as: human actors, organisational units, communication processes, documented information, work procedures and processes, technical units, human-computer interactions, and competencies that interact to pursue a common goal and criterion.


Trist and Bamforth (1951), Cherns (1976, 1987), Berniker (1992, 1996), Clegg (2002), Trist (1981), Fox (1995), Hermann (2009), Walker et.al.(2008)
«Every organisation could be viewed as the sociotechnical system that made up of people and knowledge (the social system) using tools and equipment (the technical system) to produce goods or services»

STS properties
Emergent properties
Properties of the system of as a whole that depend on the system components and their relationships

Non-deterministic
They do not always produce the same output when presented with the same input because the systems’s behaviour is partially dependent on human operators and other stochastic attributes of used technologies

Complex relationships with organisational objectives
How well a system supports organisational objectives depends not only on the system itself, but also on how and in what context it is used.

RTU, 2026
=== Slide 31 ===
Emergent properties
Properties of the system as a whole rather than properties that can be derived from the properties of components of a system

Emergent properties are a consequence of the relationships between system components

They can therefore only be assessed and measured once all the components have been integrated into goal system

Some emergent properties - reliability, performance, safety, and security

STS Requirements and Objectives
STS Objectives is a part of the requirements definition phase to establish a set of overall objectives that the system should meet

Examples:
“To provide a intrusion detection system for the call center that will provide internal and external warning of threats and/or unauthorised intrusion.”

“To ensure that the normal functioning of the work carried out in the call center is not seriously disrupted by events such as threats and/or unauthorised intrusion.”

STS Requirements and Objectives*
Complex systems are usually developed to address complex problems:
Problems that are not fully understood
Changes while the system is being specified

Must anticipate hardware/communications developments over the lifetime of the system

Hard to define non-functional requirements (organizational) without knowing the component structure of the system

=== Slide 36 ===
STS Design
Requirements partitioning to hardware, software and human components may involve a lot of negotiation

Difficult design problems are often assumed to be readily solved using software – a questionable assumption

Hidden and unanticipated factors of used technologies usually are not respected

Interdisciplinary activity: teams drawn from various backgrounds – common understanding and communication «language» are necessary

Constraints posed by the environment and other systems limit design choices, therefore requirements engineering possibilities are critical for the goal system design

=== Slide 37 ===
STS Modelling
System modelling helps the analyst to understand the concept (qualitative assessment) and functionality (quantitative assessment) of the system

Various models present the system from different perspectives

External perspective (Concept: Information Objects, Links)
showing the system’s context and environment (optional)

Behavioural perspective (Functionality: Tasks, Interaction, Timeline, Links)
showing the behaviour of the system

Structural perspective (Data level architecture: Data Dictionaries, Links)
showing the system or data architecture

STS Sub-systems (embedded) development
Typically, parallel projects are developed for sub-systems design

May involve some COTS  (Commercial Off-the-Shelf) systems procurement (limited adaptability)

Lack of communication across implementation teams

Bureaucratic and slow mechanism for proposing system changes means that the development schedule may be extended because of the need for rework or additional alignment

=== Slide 39 ===
STS Components integration
The process of putting hardware, software and people together to make a system

Should be tackled incrementally step by step so that sub-systems are integrated one at a time

Interface problems between sub-systems are usually found at this stage

May be problems with uncoordinated deliveries of system components

=== Slide 40 ===
STS Installation/Adaptation
After completion, the system has to be installed/adapted in the customer’s environment:

Environmental assumptions may be incorrect, i.e. physical installation problems (e.g. cabling/reflection/interference problems)

May be human resistance to the introduction of a new system

System can’t get along with other STS for some time

Staff training or change has to be identified
40
RTU, 2026
=== Slide 41 ===
STS Maintenance/Evolution
Large systems are expensive and therefore have a long lifetime. They must evolve to meet changing requirements

Evolution is inherently costly:

Changes must be analysed from a technical and business perspective
Sub-systems interact so unanticipated problems can arise
System structure is corrupted as changes are made to it

Existing systems which must be maintained are sometimes called legacy systems
41
RTU, 2026
=== Slide 42 ===
STS Verification/Validation
Must be done after each step in STS Life cycle: Conceptualization, Design, Installation, Maintenance

Verification - Does the system work as the designer intended?

Validation - Does the system work as specified in the requirements?

Qualitative and quantitative assessment could be done

Critical tasks in successful STS design and introduction

STS – the set of technologies
STS is the set of interactive technologies

Today the attribute of practically any new or existing technology/project is some digital technology which is the backbone of any other technology or whole system

Sustainability of STS depends on sustainability of embedded technologies and especially of digital technologies used

Digital technologies are specific part of technologies with high proportion of uncertainty influenced by hidden and unanticipated factors
