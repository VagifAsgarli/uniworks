  APA 7: In-text Citations and References - All on One Page
    A practical example: see how a source is cited in the text and how the same source appears in the References list.

Sample text
Open science is not only about free access to publications; it is a broader approach to the creation,
dissemination, and reuse of knowledge. UNESCO (2021) associates it with greater accessibility of scientific
knowledge, data, methods, and infrastructure. A similar institutional approach is used by the European
Commission (European Research Executive Agency [REA], n.d.).

The concept has several interpretations. Fecher and Friesike (2014) distinguish five schools of thought in open
science. Changes in research culture require not only technical tools but also changes in publication and
evaluation practices (Nosek et al., 2015). When one statement is supported by several sources, the citations
are separated by semicolons (Fecher & Friesike, 2014; UNESCO, 2021).

The structure of research also matters: a clear research question, evaluation of sources, and argumentation
are fundamental requirements for high-quality research (Booth et al., 2016). When authors are named as part
of the sentence, write Fecher and Friesike (2014); when the citation is entirely in parentheses, write (Fecher &
Friesike, 2014).

  Quick rules                                                            For a direct quotation
  1 author/organization: (UNESCO, 2021)                                  One page: (Author, 2021, p. 27)
  2 authors: (Fecher & Friesike, 2014)                                   Several pages: (Author, 2021, pp. 27-29)
  3+ authors: (Nosek et al., 2015)                                       References: alphabetical order + hanging indent.
  Several sources: (A, 2020; B, 2021)

Italics matter: italicize the title of a book, report, or webpage; for a journal article, italicize the journal title and volume number. The
title of a journal article or book chapter is not italicized. Write a DOI in URL format.
